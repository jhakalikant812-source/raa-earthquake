Why Earthquakes Happen in India (Seismic Zones Explained)

India is one of the most earthquake-prone countries in the world due to its unique geographical location. The Indian tectonic plate is constantly moving and colliding with the Eurasian plate, which leads to frequent seismic activity.

Understanding Tectonic Movement in India

The Indian plate is slowly pushing northwards and colliding with the Eurasian plate. This collision is responsible for the formation of the Himalayas and also causes earthquakes.

Seismic Zones in India

India is divided into four seismic zones based on earthquake risk:

  • Zone II: Low risk
  • Zone III: Moderate risk
  • Zone IV: High risk
  • Zone V: Very high risk

High Risk Areas in India

  • Himalayan region
  • Northeast India
  • Parts of Gujarat
  • Delhi and surrounding regions

Why the Himalayas Are Highly Active

The Himalayas are still rising due to tectonic collision, making the region unstable. This is why earthquakes frequently occur in this area.

Recent Earthquake Trends in India

India has experienced several moderate earthquakes in recent years, highlighting the need for awareness and preparedness among citizens.

Importance of Preparedness

People living in high-risk zones should follow safety guidelines and stay informed. Proper building construction and disaster planning can reduce damage.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Which zone is most dangerous?
Zone V has the highest earthquake risk.

Is Delhi earthquake-prone?
Yes, it lies in a moderate to high-risk zone.

Why are earthquakes common in the Himalayas?
Due to active tectonic plate collision.
